P0043	[normal]	Overview of the current system
P0045	[normal]	The scheduling of laboratory assistants has become increasingly important due` to the growing complexity of academic programs and the need to efficiently meet course requirements and student availability (Shelar et al., 2025). In many higher education institutions, including the College of Computer Studies at Central Philippine University (CPU), manual scheduling methods using spreadsheets remain the primary approach. Department administrators and administrative staff manually input assistant availability, assign shifts, and resolve scheduling conflicts through Microsoft Excel. This traditional approach heavily depends on personal judgment and experience, which often leads to human errors, overlapping assignments, and unequal workload distribution. Similar laboratory requirements and overlapping time slots increase the difficulty of producing accurate and balanced schedules (Shelar et al., 2025).
P0046	[normal]	The key feature of this system is its ability to directly address the specific problems caused by manual scheduling at CPU's College of Computer Studies. Overlapping assignments are eliminated through hard constraints enforced by the CP-SAT solver, which ensures that no instructor or student assistant is scheduled in two places at the same time and that no room is double booked during the same period. Unequal workload distribution is addressed in the student assistant scheduling phase, where the system first maximizes the number of sessions covered and then distributes the remaining workload evenly across available assistants based on their class schedules, room qualifications, and weekly hour limits. Human error is reduced by removing manual input from the scheduling process entirely. Administrators configure the rules once and the system generates a schedule that satisfies all constraints automatically. When sudden changes occur such as a student assistant withdrawal, administrators can update the availability and regenerate the schedule without having to manually check every affected slot.

P0048	[normal]	The College of Computer Science uses laboratory assistants to open the laboratories used in classes and provide technical assistance during laboratory-based classes. P0052	[normal]	Laboratory room and assistant scheduling at the College of Computer Studies in Central Philippine University (CPU) is done manually using spreadsheets, which can lead to errors such as overlapping schedules and incorrect assignments.
P0053	[normal]	The current scheduling process does not automatically enforce constraints such as assistant availability and maximum workload limits, which may result in an unequal distribution of laboratory duties among assistants.
P0054	[normal]	It is difficult to quickly arrange a replacement schedule when a laboratory assistant is absent, since there is no system that automatically identifies and assigns an available reliever for the duty hours.

The goal of the model is to ensure the workloads were evenly and fairly distributed while still complying with hospital regulations. It shows that the approach successfully produced fair and balanced schedules. This study supports the use of lexicographic multi-criteria sorting, as it shows the effectiveness of lexicographic goal programming for personnel scheduling multiple competing  criteria, directly applicable to scheduling laboratory assistants where fair workload distribution must be satisfied in a strict priority order.
P0149	[normal]	Abdulov et al. (2024) developed an automated scheduling system designed to generate optimized timetables without conflicts for academic institutions. The system considered multiple constraints, including room availability, instructor availability, and student preferences, to produce balanced and efficient timetables. Results showed that automating the creation of timetables reduced the time and effort used by staff while improving the overall quality and fairness of the schedules. This study supports the present study's goal of replacing manual scheduling with an automated system, as it shows how applying optimization algorithms and constraint handling in academic scheduling effectively eliminates conflicts and improves resource allocation.
P0151	[normal]	Wang et al. (2024) conducted a study to use an approach to solve the employee scheduling problem that uses hard and soft constraints in assigning employee shifts. The study used a hard constraint control mechanism to enforce scheduling rules such as maximum working days and a strategy to accommodate soft constraints related to individual work time preferences and workload demands. The approach used a pairwise allocation strategy to find suitable employee combinations while following scheduling rules. Results showed that the method was more effective and efficient than existing baseline approaches in producing balanced schedules. This information is relevant to the study as it supports the idea of using hard and soft constraints in automated scheduling, which is the same method used in the proposed system.

The Admin manages instructors, which extends to setting instructor availability windows, applying floor restrictions for instructors who cannot access upper floors, assigning subjects to instructors, and setting a preferred start time per assignment. These extensions mean that managing an instructor is not just adding a name, it covers all the constraints the solver needs to generate a valid and preference-aware schedule. P0203	[normal]	The Student Assistant logs in and views their assigned schedule. Their assignments are produced by the solver after checking their blocked timeslots, verifying they are within their weekly hour limit, and distributing duties fairly across all available assistants.
P0204	[normal]	Sequence Diagram of the Proposed System 
                                Figure 5. Sequence Diagram
P0205	[normal]	The sequence diagram presents the step-by-step interaction between the Admin, Instructor, Student Assistant, Frontend, Flask API, Database, and CP-SAT Solver across five main processes.
P0207	[normal]	Authentication
P0208	[normal]	The process begins when the Admin, Instructor, or Student Assistant logs into the system through the frontend. The frontend sends the credentials to the Flask API, which checks them against the database. Once validated, the user role is returned and the user is redirected to their respective dashboard.
P0210	[normal]	Configuration
P0211	[normal]	After authentication, the Admin configures all scheduling data by adding subjects, rooms, instructors, and student assistants through the frontend. The frontend sends each entry to the Flask API, which stores the data in the database and confirms that it was saved successfully.
P0213	[normal]	Phase 1: Instructor Scheduling
P0214	[normal]	The Admin triggers the solver by clicking Run Solver. The frontend sends a request to the Flask API, which fetches all required data from the database and passes it to the CP-SAT Solver. The solver generates candidate blocks, applies constraints such as room type, capacity, floor restrictions, and availability, then runs in two stages. Stage 1 finds a feasible solution and Stage 2 minimizes deviation from the instructor's preferred time. The resulting assignments are returned to the API and saved to the database.
P0216	[normal]	Phase 2: SA Assignment
P0217	[normal]	Immediately after Phase 1, the API triggers the SA assignment pass. The solver runs lexicographic multi-criteria sorting in two stages — Stage 1 maximizes the number of sessions covered by available student assistants, and Stage 2 balances the workload evenly among them. The SA assignments are returned, saved to the database, and the complete schedule is displayed to the Admin.
P0219	[normal]	Schedule Viewing
P0220	[normal]	The Instructor logs in and requests their schedule. The frontend fetches only the sessions assigned to that instructor from the API and displays them. The Student Assistant logs in and requests their assigned sessions. The frontend fetches only the SA assignments linked to that student assistant and displays them.
P0224	[normal]	Dynamic Rescheduling
P0225	[normal]	When a change occurs, the Admin updates the relevant data and triggers a rerun. The frontend clears the existing schedule and sends a new solver request. The API repeats Phase 1 and Phase 2 with the updated data and returns a new conflict-free schedule for display.

P0258	[normal]	System Overview
P0260	[normal]	Laboratory room and assistant scheduling at the College of Computer Studies is done manually using spreadsheets. This often leads to errors like overlapping schedules, wrong room assignments, and uneven workloads among assistants. There is no system that automatically checks rules like assistant availability or workload limits. Everything depends on the coordinator to catch mistakes.
P0262	[normal]	The problems this causes are real. A scheduling conflict can disrupt an entire laboratory session. An overloaded assistant affects the quality of help that students receive. When an assistant is absent, finding a replacement means manually checking who is free, with no guarantee it gets resolved quickly. The current process is slow, error-prone, and puts too much pressure on the people managing it.
P0264	[normal]	This system was built to fix that. It automatically assigns instructors, subjects, rooms, and student assistants to a weekly schedule. All rules are applied by the system. No instructor gets double-booked. Rooms are matched to the correct subject type. Sessions are placed within the instructor's available hours. Assistant workload is spread evenly across all assignments.
P0266	[normal]	The system runs in two phases. The first phase builds the instructor and room schedule. It uses a CP-SAT solver from Google OR-Tools to apply hard rules and find the best match for each instructor's preferred time. The second phase assigns student assistants to the finished schedule. It uses lexicographic multi-criteria sorting, which means it first tries to cover as many sessions as possible, then spreads the workload fairly among assistants based on their class schedules, room qualifications, and hour limits.
P0268	[normal]	The system is a desktop application built with Electron, React, TypeScript, Flask, and SQLite. It is designed to replace the spreadsheet process at CPU's College of Computer Studies with something faster, more reliable, and easier to manage.

P0281	[normal]	The Instructors page manages the instructors assigned to subjects in the schedule. An instructor is added by entering their name and selecting a floor restriction if applicable. The result is displayed as a timetable grid organized by day, with time slots as rows and rooms as columns. Each filled cell shows the subject code, subject name, and assigned instructor. The schedule can be cleared at any time to allow a fresh run.
P0288	[normal]	Physical Environment
P0290	[normal]	The system is a desktop application, so it does not need an internet connection or a remote server to work. Everything runs on the local machine.
P0292	[normal]	Electron holds the whole application together. It packages the React frontend and the Flask backend into a single program that can be installed and opened like any regular desktop app. When the application starts, Electron automatically launches Flask in the background so the scheduling logic and database operations are ready to go. Closing the application also shuts Flask down cleanly. The database is just a SQLite file sitting on the same machine, so there is nothing extra to install or configure on that side.
P0294	[normal]	To get the project running during development, the machine needs Node.js and npm for the frontend, Python 3 and pip for the backend, and three Python packages that can be installed from the included requirements file. Those packages are Flask 3.0.3, Flask-CORS 4.0.1, and OR-Tools 9.10.4067. The OR-Tools package is what powers the CP-SAT solver.
P0296	[normal]	The application can be built for Windows, macOS, and Linux. Windows produces a portable executable and an MSI installer, macOS produces a DMG file, and Linux produces an AppImage. The way Flask is shut down differs slightly between platforms. Windows uses a force kill command while macOS and Linux use a termination signal. Outside of that the codebase works the same across all three.

P0304	[normal]	The software specifications identify the tools and technologies used in the development and deployment of the Automated Laboratory Scheduling System. These technologies support the desktop interface, backend data management, scheduling logic, and constraint-based optimization.
P0308	[normal]	Table 2. Software Specifications
P0311	[normal]	Operating System: Windows 10 or later, macOS 11 or later, Linux x64
P0313	[normal]	The system is built using Electron, which supports Windows, macOS, and Linux out of the box. The build scripts in the project produce a portable executable and MSI installer for Windows, a DMG file for macOS running on Apple Silicon, and an AppImage for Linux. Since all core technologies used in the system are cross-platform, the application can be developed and run on any of the three operating systems without requiring changes to the codebase.
P0315	[normal]	Desktop Framework: Electron 42.0.1
P0317	[normal]	Electron is an open-source framework developed by GitHub that allows developers to build cross-platform desktop applications using web technologies . It combines a Chromium-based renderer with a Node.js runtime, enabling the use of HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and TypeScript for building native desktop interfaces.
P0319	[normal]	In this system, Electron serves as the application shell that bundles the React frontend and the Flask backend into a single installable program. When the application is launched in production, Electron automatically spawns the Flask server as a background process and shuts it down cleanly when the application is closed. This setup removes the need for users to manually start or configure any backend service.
P0321	[normal]	Front-End: Vite, React 19.2.5, TypeScript 6.0.2
P0323	[normal]	React is an open-source JavaScript library developed by Meta for building user interfaces through a component-based architecture. TypeScript is a statically typed superset of JavaScript developed by Microsoft that adds type safety and improved tooling to JavaScript projects . Vite is a modern frontend build tool that provides fast development server startup and optimized production builds.
P0325	[normal]	The frontend of the system is built using React and TypeScript, with Vite handling the development server and build process. The interface is organized into four main tabs covering subjects, rooms, instructors, and the generated schedule. All data is fetched from the Flask backend through a typed API client, and the schedule results are displayed as an interactive timetable grid.
P0327	[normal]	Back-End: Python, Flask 3.0.3, Flask-CORS 4.0.1
P0329	[normal]	Flask is a lightweight web framework for Python developed by Armin Ronacher that follows a minimalist design philosophy and provides the tools needed to build web applications and REST APIs . Flask-CORS is an extension that handles cross-origin resource sharing between the Electron renderer process and the Flask server during development.
P0331	[normal]	The Flask backend serves as the bridge between the frontend and the scheduling logic. It exposes REST API endpoints for managing subjects, rooms, instructors, availability windows, room types, and schedules. All data is read from and written to a local SQLite database, and the solver is triggered through a dedicated endpoint that passes all required data to the CP-SAT model.
P0333	[normal]	Constraint Solver: Google OR-Tools 9.10.4067, CP-SAT
P0335	[normal]	Google OR-Tools is an open-source software suite developed by Google for solving combinatorial optimization problems, including constraint programming, linear programming, and vehicle routing. The CP-SAT solver within OR-Tools uses a combination of constraint propagation and branch-and-bound search to find optimal or near-optimal solutions efficiently.
P0337	[normal]	The CP-SAT solver is the core of the scheduling system. It receives all configured data including subjects, rooms, instructors, availability windows, floor restrictions, and preferred times, and produces a conflict-free schedule that satisfies all hard constraints. The solver operates in two stages, first finding any feasible solution and then minimizing deviation from each instructor's preferred start time.
P0340	[normal]	Application Packaging: electron-builder 24.7.0
P0342	[normal]	electron-builder is an open-source tool for packaging and distributing Electron applications across multiple platforms. It handles code signing, installer generation, and platform-specific build configurations.
P0344	[normal]	In this system, electron-builder is configured to produce platform-specific distributable files for Windows, macOS, and Linux. The Python backend files are included as extra resources in the packaged application, ensuring the Flask server and all required scripts are available when the installed application is launched on an end user's machine.
P0346	[normal]	Database: SQLite
P0348	[normal]	SQLite is a self-contained, serverless, and zero-configuration relational database engine that stores the entire database as a single file on disk . It is widely used in desktop and embedded applications where a lightweight and portable database solution is needed.
P0350	[normal]	SQLite was chosen for this system because it requires no separate installation or server configuration. The database file is created automatically when the application is first launched and stores all scheduling data including subjects, rooms, instructors, availability windows, room types, and generated schedules. This makes the system easy to set up and portable across machines.

P0384	[normal]	Tangible and Intangible Benefits
P0386	[normal]	Tangible Benefits
P0388	[normal]	The system reduces the time required to manually create and manage laboratory schedules. The system automatically assigns instructors, rooms, and student assistants to sessions based on configured constraints, removing the need for manual spreadsheet-based scheduling. The system enforces hard constraints such as no instructor overlap, no room double-booking, room type and capacity matching, and instructor availability, ensuring that every generated schedule is conflict-free. The system stores all scheduling data and generated schedules in a centralized local database, making records easy to access and manage.
P0390	[normal]	Intangible Benefits
P0392	[normal]	The system improves the overall efficiency of laboratory scheduling at the College of Computer Studies. It reduces the likelihood of human error that comes with manual spreadsheet-based scheduling. The system supports fair distribution of student assistant workload, which reduces the risk of overburdening individual assistants. It also supports better coordination between instructors and student assistants through the built-in messaging feature and improves transparency by giving each user role access to the schedule information relevant to them.
P0394	[normal]	Design and Implementation Issues
P0396	[normal]	The development of the Automated Laboratory Scheduling System may encounter several design and implementation issues. First, the accuracy of the generated schedule depends entirely on the quality of the data entered by the administrator. Incomplete availability windows, missing room type assignments, or incorrect student counts may cause the solver to return an infeasible result or produce a schedule that does not reflect actual requirements.
P0398	[normal]	Second, the CP-SAT solver operates within a 30-second time limit per run. For larger datasets with many instructor-subject pairs and tight availability constraints, the solver may return a feasible but not fully optimized solution within that time limit.
P0400	[normal]	Third, the student assistant assignment phase depends on the instructor schedule being generated first. If Phase 1 fails, Phase 2 cannot run. This means all data must be correctly configured before a complete schedule can be produced.
P0402	[normal]	Fourth, the system currently runs as a desktop application on a single machine. Since SQLite is a file-based database with no network access, instructors and student assistants cannot view their schedules from their own devices until a login system and role-based access are implemented in the final version.
P0404	[normal]	Fifth, dynamic rescheduling currently requires the administrator to manually clear the existing schedule and rerun the solver. Partial rescheduling that locks confirmed sessions and only reassigns affected ones is planned for future implementation.
P0406	[normal]	Lastly, system performance may be affected by the number of instructor-subject pairs, the complexity of availability constraints, and the available hardware resources of the machine running the application. Machines with limited processing power may experience longer solver run times, particularly when the model has many candidate blocks to evaluate.
